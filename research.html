Research

I study what limited, noisy time-series data can reveal about the hidden dynamics behind them, and how modeling assumptions, regularization, and estimation algorithms decide the answer.

Publication

A physics-informed neural network framework for inferring infinitesimal phase response curves from finite-pulse circadian recordings

D. Won*, G. Hwang*, and J. K. Kim · Nature Communications, accepted (2026) · bioRxiv | code

I led the development, implementation, and simulations of PINN-iPRC, which infers infinitesimal phase response curves (iPRCs) from finite-pulse phase trajectories using phase-equation constraints. In three Arabidopsis mutants, the inferred iPRCs correlated with singularity-response estimates (Pearson r = 0.865–0.987). On the shorter synthetic records tested, it reduced error relative to the Cestnik–Rosenblum method.

Ongoing projects

Latent representation and dynamics identifiability

With Y. Kim and W. Ha · August 2026 – present · Manuscript in preparation

We study when latent states and shared affine dynamics can be identified from nonlinear observations, using known targets and observed state–velocity laws from noisy multi-node interventions. Under full state coverage, separating targets, and full conditional noise variation, we prove identifiability up to coordinate scaling, given suitable links and a shared origin.

Early stopping for Poisson EM

With W. Ha (advisor) · March 2026 – present · Manuscript in preparation

We developed finite-time KL bounds for full-step Poisson EM from a single histogram, separating source-approximation, acquisition-depth, iteration, and photon-count costs. I analyzed noise amplification across observation directions and the conditions under which early stopping outperforms sufficiently late EM iterates.

Initial-condition estimation in epidemic models (Hist-LQ)

Co-first author with D. Lim; with S. Choi, S. W. Park, and J. K. Kim · December 2025 – present · Manuscript in preparation

I extended constant history priors to linear and quadratic ones (Hist-LQ) and implemented the estimator. I benchmarked it against eight methods on simulations and COVID-19/MERS data, using 10–14-day windows.

Earlier project

Optimizer-based temporal network clustering

Advisor: H. Chun (KAIST) · Fall 2023 – Fall 2024

I implemented variational inference for dynamic stochastic block models in PyTorch, using tensor operations, gradient optimizers, and softmax/sigmoid reparameterizations. In benchmarks on 100 simulated networks per setting, it obtained higher adjusted Rand indices; per-iteration time was about 0.03 s, versus about 0.08 s for tensorized fixed-point VEM.
